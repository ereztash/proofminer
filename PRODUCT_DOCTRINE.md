# ProofMiner — Product Doctrine

## Telos
ProofMiner exists to help an expert turn **already-earned evidence** into a **specific change in a buyer's belief**.

The product is successful when the user can answer, faster and with more confidence:

> What should I show this audience now, what will it make them believe, and what is the next action?

A LinkedIn post is one possible representation of that decision. It is not the product's core value.

## Primary user
An independent expert, consultant, freelancer, coach, therapist, or small professional-services business who already has real work, experience, clients, outcomes, talks, publications, credentials, testimonials, or external mentions — but whose evidence is fragmented and underused.

They do **not** primarily need more ideas. They need help selecting, interpreting and deploying what they already earned.

## Core job
1. Gather existing evidence with minimal cognitive load.
2. Break containers (CV, article, testimonial, transcript, case study) into atomic Proof Units.
3. Ask what belief/uncertainty matters for the current buyer.
4. Rank proof by its expected ability to move that belief — not by prestige alone.
5. Explain why one item wins, what uncertainty remains, and what would strengthen it.
6. Turn the chosen proof into useful professional communication without inventing facts.

## The decision model
The system must separate:
- **Evidence strength** — how verifiable, specific and independent the evidence is.
- **Audience fit** — how directly it addresses the buyer's current uncertainty.
- **Commercial proximity** — how close the evidence is to the outcome/offer being sold.
- **Confidence** — how complete the available evidence is.

Do not present heuristic scores as objective truth. A score is a prioritization aid.

## Proof hierarchy
Higher-priority evidence usually has more of the following:
- observed outcome > self-description
- named/specific result > vague experience
- third-party validation > self-assertion
- buyer-relevant evidence > prestigious but irrelevant credential
- causal proximity to the offer > distant status signal
- recent/contextual evidence > stale evidence when recency matters

This is not absolute; audience and goal can reverse the ranking.

## UX invariant
**The user's attention is a scarce resource.**

At every screen, prioritize in this order:
1. What should I do now?
2. Why is this the best next move?
3. What belief will it change?
4. What evidence supports the recommendation?
5. Only then: model details, dimensions and diagnostics.

Progressive disclosure is mandatory. Do not make users understand the scoring model before receiving value.

## Language invariant
Use the user's language, not the system's taxonomy.

Prefer:
- "מה הכי שווה להראות עכשיו?"
- "מה זה יגרום ללקוח להבין?"
- "מה חסר כדי שזה יהיה משכנע יותר?"
- "מאיפה ההוכחה הזו מגיעה?"

Avoid exposing terms such as ontology, evidence graph, conversion proximity, Proof Unit, or ICP unless they genuinely help the user make a decision. Internal precision does not justify external cognitive cost.

## LinkedIn invariant
Generated content must provide professional value beyond self-promotion. Evidence is the anchor; the post should add context, lesson, implication, or a useful point of view. Never fabricate facts, metrics, clients, causality, or third-party validation.

## Visual invariant
Visual design serves attention allocation, not decoration.
- One dominant action/result per viewport.
- Strong typographic hierarchy.
- Quiet neutral background; one restrained action color.
- Numbers only when they help compare or decide.
- Avoid dashboard density before the user has a decision to make.
- Use whitespace to separate decisions, not to create visual luxury.
- Mobile must preserve the same priority order.

## Anti-build
Do not add scheduling, virality scores, hashtag generators, broad analytics, engagement automation, or content calendars until field evidence shows they improve the core job.

## Field success metrics
Early MVP metrics, in order:
1. **Discovery rate:** user identifies at least one proof they had not considered deployable.
2. **Decision rate:** user can choose one proof to use now without external help.
3. **Belief clarity:** user can state what buyer belief the selected proof is meant to change.
4. **Deployment rate:** selected proof becomes a real external artifact/post/message/profile update.
5. **Downstream signal:** conversation, profile visit, reply, qualified lead, or sales movement attributable enough to learn from.

Impressions are secondary unless the goal explicitly requires reach.

## Stop rule for refinement
Do not continue improving the internal model merely because more refinement is possible. Continue only when a disagreement between perspectives changes:
- the user decision,
- the product action,
- the evidence requirement,
- or the field test.

When independent review perspectives agree on the same next product decision and remaining disagreement no longer changes the field test, ship the test.
