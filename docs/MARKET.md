# MARKET — what the outside says, and what changes because of it

`TELOS.md` holds the conclusions. This holds the work underneath them, the two
findings that only appear when separate lines of evidence are laid on top of
each other, and — the point of the document — **the plan for turning any of it
into a change to this repository.**

It is written to be falsifiable and to be thrown away. If the five-person trial
contradicts it, the trial wins.

## Method, and what it could not reach

Five parallel research passes were run against the outside world: the
competitive and pricing landscape; valuation and exit comparables; the
organisational claim-substantiation market; the free-tool-to-paid-practice
pattern; and Israeli market sizing.

**Four returned. The sizing pass did not.** Every Israeli figure below therefore
rests on a single secondary source rather than two independent ones, and is
marked where it is load-bearing. That is the weakest link in this document and
it is named here rather than buried.

Three things were searched for and **do not exist**, which is itself a result:

- **No independent data on free-diagnostic → paid-engagement conversion.** None.
  Every number circulating in that industry is an opt-in rate. The category
  leader, holding eighty million leads of data, publishes no revenue conversion
  figure at all, and the foundational "interactive content converts twice as
  well" claim traces to a vendor-sponsored survey of marketer *opinion*.
- **No controlled comparison of a gated versus ungated professional diagnostic.**
  Nobody has measured it.
- **No post-mortem of a verbatim-extraction product for individuals**, because
  no such product was found. That is an empty space, not a proven one, and the
  difference matters.

So parts of what follows rest on mechanism and inference rather than
measurement. Those parts are marked.

## The finding that only appears in combination

No single pass produced this. Three did, from different directions, without
knowing about each other.

**Where evidence is bought, someone is liable if it is wrong.**

- Employment-verification vendors raise real money — the **employer** pays.
  B2B customer-proof platforms raise real money — the **marketing team** pays.
  The one portfolio-of-evidence company that exited well sold to **institutions**.
- Regulated claim substantiation is a multi-billion-dollar software category with
  a thirty-year-old incumbent, because a regulator, a penalty schedule and a
  named budget owner all exist.
- Employer-brand substantiation has no regulator, no penalty and no budget owner.
  It is not a market. Content provenance is becoming free infrastructure for the
  same reason.

Laid together:

> **Willingness to pay for evidence is proportional to the liability of getting
> it wrong. An individual professional bears no liability for overstating
> themselves.**

That one sentence explains both halves of the graveyard. The same core mechanic
— score a claim against the evidence beneath it — died as a consumer product and
thrives as a compliance product, and the only variable that differs is who pays
when the claim is false.

**And it lands on this product directly.** The Liebig gate, the refusal to
fabricate, the verbatim slice: that is liability infrastructure, and the actor
this product is built for carries no liability. It is a seatbelt sold to someone
who is not driving.

*Confidence: high on each input; the synthesis is mine and is an inference.*

## Which names a buyer the product did not know it had

If the individual is not liable for their own overstatement, someone else is.
**The person who vouched for them.** The one who made the introduction, put the
name forward, staked their own standing on it.

In this practice, that person is the practitioner. Every client put in front of
somebody else carries their reputational risk, not the client's.

> **The tool is not a giveaway that generates leads. It is infrastructure that
> de-risks vouching — and the first person it de-risks is whoever runs it.**

This costs nothing to adopt, changes no code, and is the only framing found in
which the product's structure and the money are pointed at the same person.

*Confidence: medium. This is a reframe, not a measurement, and no comparable was
found for it.*

## The disagreement, and why it dissolves

Two passes reached opposite conclusions on the same question.

- One: the tool captures nothing, therefore it produces nothing; **add email
  capture immediately**.
- The other: capture would cost usage *and* credibility together; **the fix is
  measurement at intake, not capture.**

They are not in conflict. They are pricing two different assets. Capture raises
the value of the **software** — attributable, listable, saleable. Refusing
capture raises the value of the **practice** — credible, recommendable.

And the first pass had already established that the software is worth roughly
nothing either way: a working product with no users prices at the low hundreds
to low thousands, a public permissive repository pushes that toward zero, and
the two largest marketplaces would not list it at all.

> **Refusing capture gives up something worth nothing and buys the only thing
> that pays.** The rule in `TELOS.md` stands.

## What the tool is actually for

Two independent results, combined:

- Buyers of professional services reach out roughly **70% of the way through
  their own process** — around eight months into eleven — and **84% of deals go
  to the first vendor contacted.**
- Across a thousand buyers, what convinces someone that a person is an expert is
  a **colleague's recommendation (56.5%)** and **"they made a complicated
  subject seem understandable" (38.1%)** — ahead of publications and
  certifications.

> **The goal is not to convert the person using the tool. It is to be the first
> name they think of eight months later — and the mechanism is that they
> repeated a number out loud to somebody.**

Which produces the one product directive the research actually justifies:
**engineer for quotability**. A sentence a person can say to a colleague from
memory. Not conversion, not capture, not features.

**But that directive is not yet a licence to build** — see the plan below.

## The ICP cut

`TELOS.md` names two populations: people going independent, and people trying to
get hired. Three independent results say the second should go.

1. A field experiment across roughly **500,000 jobseekers** found algorithmic
   writing assistance produced **8% more hires and 10% higher wages**, with no
   measured drop in employer satisfaction. For that half, the anti-goal asks a
   person to accept a worse outcome in exchange for a principle.
2. They are the half with neither budget nor liability — the exact profile the
   liability finding says will not pay.
3. This repository already says so: `track: 'job'` has **"no representation at
   all in anything observed so far."**

Three lines of evidence, arrived at separately, pointing the same way. Carrying
both halves doubles the audience and halves the sharpness of every sentence
written for either.

**This is a recommendation to the owner, not a decision taken here.** It narrows
what the product is for, and that is not a call to make inside a research note.

# The plan

The organising constraint is already in `TELOS.md`: **build nothing before the
first person.** Research does not lift it. A finding produced by reading the
market is exactly the kind of thing that feels urgent and turns out to be about
somebody else's users.

So the plan sorts every item by what it is allowed to touch **now**.

## Now — documents and protocol only

Nothing here changes a line of application code.

| # | Change | Where | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Record the liability finding and the vouching frame | `TELOS.md` | An hour |
| 2 | Put the ICP cut to the owner as a decision, with its three supports | `TELOS.md`, beside the existing open question about which gap this is | An hour |
| 3 | Add *"what made you reach out?"* — free text, unprompted — to the intake of every future conversation | Practice, not repo | Zero |
| 4 | Add the trigger-event question to the day-0 script: *what happens in the next thirty days that makes this matter?* | Trial protocol in `TELOS.md` | Zero |
| 5 | Write the pre-registered coding rule, with a forced third bucket: definition-blocked / visibility-blocked / **neither** | Trial protocol in `TELOS.md` | An hour |
| 6 | Register the name as a trademark | External | Small, one-off |

Item 3 is the whole answer to the only real cost of the no-capture rule. Without
capture there is no way to tell *working slowly and invisibly* apart from *not
working* — the condition under which people kill a working asset or keep feeding
a dead one. Self-reported attribution is the instrument that closes that gap;
one published study found software attribution missing **90%** of what buyers
themselves credited.

Item 5's third bucket exists because the temptation at coding time is to score
an ambiguous transcript as visibility-blocked, which is the reading that
confirms the product.

**Items 1–5 are discharged, all of them in `TELOS.md`**: the liability finding
and the vouching frame under *How this is worth money*; the ICP cut beside the
open question about which gap this is; the intake question under the capture
rule; the trigger-event question in the day-0 script; the coding rule and its
third bucket in the falsifier. Item 6 is not a repository change and is still
open. **Item 2 remains a decision the owner has not taken** — writing it down is
not taking it.

## During the trial — what to observe

The research produced claims about behaviour. The trial is the instrument that
can test them, and adding an observation costs nothing while building for one
costs everything.

| Observation | Tests | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Did they repeat a number to anybody?** Unprompted, at day 14: *have you said any of this out loud to someone since?* | The quotability directive. If nobody repeats anything, engineering for it is wasted work | Day 14 |
| **Did they correct the extraction unprompted?** *"No, it was twenty-one days, not nineteen"* | Ownership of the evidence. A correction is a far stronger signal than agreement — this repository's own negative test set says agreement is not confirmation | Day 0 |
| **Which population did they come from?** | The ICP cut. If the independents and the jobseekers fail differently, that is the answer | Day 0, coded |
| **What was the trigger event?** | Whether this pain has a date on it, or is chronic and therefore unbudgeted | Day 0 |

The first of these is the important one, and it is the disciplined form of the
research's main product finding: **do not build for quotability — measure
whether it happens.** If two or more of the five repeated a number to somebody
without being asked to, the directive is live and the trial has already shown
what they quoted. If nobody did, the directive is dead and no code was written
for it.

**All four now sit in the day-0 and day-14 scripts in `TELOS.md`,** with the
day-14 questions in a fixed order because three of them prime each other. An
observation that lives only in a research note is not an observation — it is an
intention that the person running the conversation will not have in front of
them, and every one of these is unrecoverable the day after.

## After — changes with their trigger conditions

Nothing in this column begins until the trial concludes. Each item names the
result that unlocks it, so that none of them can be started on enthusiasm.

| Change | Unlocked by |
|---|---|
| Engineer the gap number for quotability | ≥2 of 5 repeated a number unprompted at day 14 |
| Narrow the product to the independent track in code and copy | The two populations failed differently, **and** the owner takes the ICP decision |
| Build the paid rung between the free tool and the sprint | The trial is green — 4/5 on criteria 1, 2, 4 and 3/5 on criterion 3 |
| Take the organisational fork | The trial says the individual gap is real, **and** a deliberate decision that the practice is not the business. See the three standing reasons for "not now" in `TELOS.md` |
| Anything at all about certification, licensing or a course | An audience and published client results exist. In every documented case the book and the audience came first; certification harvests demand and cannot create it |

## What is explicitly not on the plan

- **Selling the software.** A working product with no users prices in the low
  hundreds; the two main marketplaces will not list it; a public permissive
  repository removes the exclusivity that pre-revenue pricing is based on.
  Expected value is under a thousand, against thirty to sixty hours of work.
- **Adding accounts, telemetry or a subscription.** Contradicts the two refusals
  and deletes the credibility that makes the tool worth pasting into.
- **Adding email capture.** See above.
- **Building features before the trial.** The plan's organising constraint.

## What would falsify this document

Two conditions, both observable, neither dependent on anyone's judgement:

1. **Twelve months after the tool is in front of people, if the intake question
   from item 3 has never once produced an answer that mentions it** — the
   demand-creation mechanism this entire document rests on is not running here.
   The no-capture rule was then the wrong call, and the correct response is to
   say so rather than to wait longer.
2. **If the trial is green and the paid rung still does not sell** — the problem
   was never the diagnostic, and the ladder described in `TELOS.md` is a
   comfortable story rather than a business.

A commercial plan with no result that would refute it is a plan to feel busy.

## What surprised the author

The research was commissioned to answer *what should this cost*. It answered a
different question instead: **who here is actually at risk if the claim is
wrong** — and on the current design, nobody is.

That question should have been asked before a line of this was written. It is
the more useful one, and it is now the first thing in this file.
