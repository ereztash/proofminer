# 1. O recalled

The end-to-end telos remains:

> Increase the likelihood that a relevant audience comes to perceive the user as a credible, differentiated authority and acts accordingly, by helping the user improve a contestable representation of the problem and allocate scarce resources toward the strongest grounded path while learning from real outcomes.

The category hypothesis is allowed to change only if it helps realize that telos and improves commercial viability.

# 2. Competitive research result

## Enterprise Decision Intelligence

### Cloverpop

Public experience:

```text
trigger detection
→ frame decision
→ recommendations
→ collaborative / governed execution
→ decision system of record
→ outcome learning
```

Customer value themes:

- structure complex / contentious decisions;
- retain traceability of what was decided and why;
- learn from prior decisions;
- reduce meetings / analysis time;
- combine humans + agents.

Public reviews indicate strong value in structured collaboration and historical decision/outcome records, with recurring complaints around high cost and some onboarding / flexibility friction.

### Aera Decision Cloud

Public UX:

```text
prioritized recommendation inbox
→ inspect context / trade-offs
→ ACCEPT / MODIFY / REJECT
→ decision record
→ skills learn from decision history
```

Important lesson:

Recommendation is an object in a continuing operating system, not a one-shot answer.

### Palantir Ontology / AIP

Public architecture:

```text
data + semantic state
→ logic
→ staged action
→ human review where required
→ operational write-back
→ decision lineage
→ learning
```

Important lesson:

Decision context compounds because the system preserves the state and lineage on which prior decisions were made.

### Planview

Public experience:

```text
strategy
→ initiative portfolio
→ capacity / funding
→ scenarios
→ allocation
→ execution
→ outcomes
→ continuous rebalancing
```

Customer reviews praise centralization, portfolio/resource visibility and reporting; common friction is complexity, implementation time and steep learning curve.

Important lesson:

The product remains relevant because strategy, capacity and outcomes continuously change. It is not retained because the user enjoys planning software.

# 3. Direct / near-direct personal decision competitors

## Decyde

Direct category collision.

Public positioning explicitly includes:

- `Decision Intelligence platform`;
- individual + business modes;
- decision wizard;
- framework selection by decision type;
- structured scoring / recommendation;
- `Decision Profile` that learns from prior decisions, goals, risk tolerance, role and history;
- decision history and scenarios.

Its public flow is roughly:

```text
one-sentence decision
→ add context
→ specialized framework
→ verdict + plan
→ decision history / Decision Profile
```

This means **Personalized Decision Intelligence is not by itself a defensible differentiator**.

## Decision Journal AI

Public flow:

```text
conversation
→ capture belief / intent before outcome
→ flag one relevant bias
→ save decision
→ return ~6 weeks later
→ compare prediction / rationale to outcome
→ calibration update
```

Public launch pricing is stated as:

- $5/month beginner;
- $20/month practitioner;
- $75/month team (planned).

Important lesson:

A general personal-decision product is priced like a low-cost productivity / self-improvement product unless attached to a more valuable recurring job.

## Clara / generic decision apps

Public subscription pricing around ~$20/month reinforces the low-price ceiling of generic consumer decision assistance.

## deciqAI / siift for founders

Founder decision software is already emerging.

- deciqAI publicly positions around high-stakes founder calls such as pricing, hiring, market entry and fundraising, with plans from ~$29/month in beta.
- siift positions as a living business model / executive filter that keeps evidence, assumptions, actions and results connected and surfaces what deserves attention now.

Important implication:

**"Decision intelligence for founders" is also not an empty wedge.**

# 5. Category decision

## Reject

> "ProofMiner is a general Personal Decision Intelligence app."

Reason:

- category is not empty;
- generic decision tools already price at ~$5–$30/month;
- broad life-decision scope weakens domain evidence and creates safety / credibility problems;
- episodic personal decisions produce weak natural retention;
- it sacrifices the authority domain where we already have a developed model and measurable economic consequence.

## Retain as architecture / category layer

> **Personal Decision Intelligence** is the correct architectural family.

Meaning:

The system maintains a longitudinal representation of:

- goals;
- boundaries;
- resources;
- assumptions / decision representation;
- prior decisions;
- evidence;
- actions;
- outcomes;
- reversals;
- personal decision patterns.

It helps the person decide under uncertainty while preserving decision authorship.

## First commercial wedge

Current strongest hypothesis:

> **Personal Decision Intelligence for independent experts / consultants / fractional leaders whose reputation is an economic asset and who are actively trying to change their market position.**

Authority-building remains the first vertical because:

1. the buyer already experiences many plausible actions competing for scarce time / money / attention;
2. wrong sequencing has visible opportunity cost;
3. reputation / positioning decisions affect revenue, pricing power and inbound opportunity;
4. existing authority services prove willingness to spend hundreds to thousands of dollars;
5. the product can remain narrow enough to build real domain intelligence;
6. our differentiated mechanism can be tested against existing authority roadmaps rather than against every human decision problem.

# 6. ICP ranking

## ICP-1 — Independent experts / consultants / fractional executives in transition

Trigger examples:

- repositioning / moving upmarket;
- entering a new authority domain;
- launching a new offer;
- trying to shift from outbound/referrals to inbound;
- experience exceeds market perception;
- many authority actions are possible but sequencing is unclear.

Decision frequency: medium-high.  
Economic stakes: high.  
Ability to pay directly: high relative to consumers.  
Domain fit: highest.  
Competitive intensity: moderate.

**Current rank: #1.**

## ICP-2 — Founder / owner-operator

Recurring decisions around product, GTM, pricing, hiring, fundraising and allocation.

Decision frequency: very high.  
Economic stakes: very high.  
Ability to pay: high.  
Competition: already strong / increasing (siift, deciqAI, mentors, generic AI).

**Current rank: #2, possible later vertical.**

## ICP-3 — Senior executive / career transition

High stakes and high willingness to pay, but lower decision frequency and greater need for privacy, organizational context and enterprise procurement when employer-funded.

**Current rank: #3.**

## ICP-4 — General life decisions

Decision variety is high but willingness to pay and retention are weak; safety surface expands dramatically.

**Current rank: reject as initial market.**

# 7. The buying trigger

Do not sell:

> "Improve your decision-making."

That is too abstract and low-urgency.

The wedge needs a **struggling moment** with economic cost.

Current trigger hypothesis:

> **"I am at a professional transition where there are too many plausible things I could do to become known / chosen for the work I want next, and I do not know which move deserves my limited resources now."**

The product should enter when the cost of choosing wrong becomes visible.

# 8. Business model hypothesis

## Important distinction

The category and the paid SKU do not have to be identical.

```text
CATEGORY
Personal Decision Intelligence

FIRST VERTICAL
Professional Authority / Market Position

PAID JOB
Continuous strategic allocation + learning
```

## Price architecture to FIELD-test

Do not declare these as validated prices.

### Tier A — Self-serve decision layer

Hypothesis: **$39–$79/month**.

Needs to deliver:

- persistent decision profile;
- authority project state;
- evidence / market learning;
- real re-planning;
- clear advantage over general ChatGPT and $20 decision journals.

Below this band the product risks being perceived as generic productivity software.

Above this band it must demonstrate measurable professional / economic value.

### Tier B — Professional Intelligence

Hypothesis: **$99–$249/month**.

For users who connect richer sources / projects and receive:

- deeper person + field research;
- recurring decision reviews;
- trigger-based re-planning;
- multiple authority projects / surfaces;
- longitudinal calibration / representation history;
- optional integrations.

This is the most interesting long-term SaaS band.

### Tier C — Assisted / concierge intelligence

Wizard-of-Oz / early paid model: **$300–$1,000+/month** or **$500–$1,500 setup + lower ongoing subscription**.

Reason:

The first real intelligence will still contain human research / judgment. Existing personal-brand and mentoring markets make this price range plausible when the output changes commercially meaningful decisions.

Do not hide human work and call it autonomous SaaS.

### Avoid initially

- outcome-based fee tied to revenue: attribution is too weak and would create recommendation incentives;
- annual contract before retention mechanism is observed;
- freemium built around unlimited generic decisions: likely attracts low-stakes use and obscures ICP learning.

# 9. Retention thesis

## Reject: daily engagement as North Star

Professional strategic decisions are not a daily habit in the same way messaging or task management is.

Manufacturing daily use would risk:

- low-value recommendations;
- alert fatigue;
- unnecessary decision churn;
- metric gaming;
- reduced user authorship.

## Retain: event-driven retention

The product should become active when one of these occurs:

### Decision trigger

A new consequential choice appears.

### Evidence trigger

New market information materially changes an assumption.

### Outcome trigger

A prior action produces / fails to produce a useful signal.

### Dependency trigger

A prerequisite is completed and a previously premature action becomes available.

### Boundary trigger

Audience, domain, role, offer or career direction materially changes.

### Scheduled review

A monthly / quarterly review is justified because allocation decisions compound over a longer horizon.

# 10. Why the user still needs the product in one year

The answer cannot be:

> "because they forgot the framework."

If that is the retention mechanism, the product has failed.

The one-year value must come from **state that becomes more useful through lived history**.

After one year the system should know, with explicit provenance:

```text
what the user wanted
what they believed
what they planned
what they chose
what they actually did
what resources it consumed
what happened
which audiences / surfaces responded
which assumptions survived
which recommendations were reversed
where the user's estimates are systematically miscalibrated
which authority mechanisms repeatedly create signal
which constraints repeatedly dominate
```

Therefore year-1 value is not a static profile.

It is **decision lineage + outcome-linked personal model**.

A new general-purpose chatbot cannot reproduce that instantly from one prompt.

---

# 11. Compounding retention asset

Call the internal concept provisionally:

## Personal Decision Model

It is not a psychometric personality score.

It is a provenance-bearing longitudinal state containing:

- active goals / project boundaries;
- representation snapshots;
- decision criteria the user actually endorses;
- resource constraints / recurring opportunity costs;
- decision history;
- rejected alternatives;
- confidence / uncertainty at decision time;
- actual execution;
- observed outcomes;
- reversals / regrets;
- field / market changes;
- calibrated patterns only after enough evidence.

### Strong retention criterion

At month 12, ask:

> **Would the next recommendation materially degrade if the user's first 11 months of decision / action / outcome history disappeared?**

If no, the product has not built a compounding retention asset.

---

# 12. Ethical / product retention invariant

Retention must come from **accumulated usefulness**, not dependency.

The product should actively improve the user's ability to understand and contest decisions.

A healthy long-term user may need fewer explanations while still valuing the system because it holds:

- external evidence;
- longitudinal memory;
- cross-decision patterns;
- trigger monitoring;
- opportunity-cost state;
- execution/outcome lineage.

Do not optimize for the user becoming less able to decide alone.

# 13. Customer experience lessons

## What to copy

### From Decision Journal AI

- conversational capture instead of forms;
- freeze belief before outcome;
- explicit delayed reflection loop;
- speak only when a bias / issue is decision-relevant.

### From Aera

- recommendation as an actionable object;
- accept / modify / reject as first-class;
- persistent decision record.

### From Cloverpop

- trigger → frame → decide → execute → learn;
- decision system of record;
- transparent why / who / outcome.

### From Palantir

- stage actions before execution;
- semantic state + decision lineage;
- human authority boundary.

### From Planview

- continuous allocation / rebalancing;
- strategy-to-outcome traceability;
- capacity / opportunity-cost visibility.

### From Insideflows

- users buy transformation / opportunity, not frameworks;
- authority is a high-value commercial vertical.

## What not to copy

- Planview-style complexity / enterprise form density;
- generic scores / verdict theater;
- fixed framework taxonomy presented as product value;
- daily engagement for its own sake;
- system-authority language such as "we made the decision for you";
- onboarding that asks the user to manually populate everything the system could research.

# 14. Differentiation after research

The following are **not** differentiators:

- personalized decision intelligence;
- decision profile;
- decision history;
- specialized frameworks;
- scenario comparison;
- structured recommendations;
- outcome reflection;
- personalized authority roadmap.

Current mechanism-level differentiation hypothesis is the composition:

> **A domain-grounded personal decision system that reconstructs the user's pre-decision representation and allocation, researches the external field, selects only the reasoning needed for the live problem, produces a contestable resource-allocation change, and learns from real execution / market outcomes without replacing user authorship.**

This may still be too broad; FIELD must test whether each clause changes value.

---

# 15. Business-model kill conditions

Kill / revise the subscription thesis if:

- meaningful decision events occur too infrequently to support recurring payment;
- users obtain most value from one initial authority map and do not return after execution;
- longitudinal history does not materially change future recommendations;
- users prefer paying for human judgment / execution rather than software decision support;
- a general AI with an exported project file gives comparable value;
- trigger monitoring does not generate decision-relevant returns;
- willingness to pay clusters near generic decision-app pricing despite authority-specific value.

Kill / revise the authority wedge if:

- users consistently describe their urgent job as content execution rather than allocation;
- authority decisions have weak observable feedback loops;
- users cannot attribute enough economic consequence to sequencing / allocation to justify recurring spend;
- the founder/business-decision wedge demonstrates materially stronger retention and willingness to pay.

---

# 16. FIELD plan — commercial validation

Before building broader Personal Decision Intelligence, run paid / willingness-to-pay tests on the authority wedge.

For each participant capture:

1. current high-stakes authority / market-position decision;
2. economic / career consequence of getting it wrong;
3. current spend on adjacent substitutes (coach, consultant, brand service, ChatGPT, content team, mentoring);
4. initial Representation + Allocation baseline;
5. personalized intervention;
6. decision / resource change;
7. declared maximum credible willingness to pay **after value is experienced**, not before;
8. offer one real paid continuation rather than only asking hypothetical price;
9. next expected decision event;
10. follow-up event / evidence / replan;
11. whether prior history changed the next recommendation.

### Price test cells

For an assisted beta, test real offers around materially different anchors rather than small cosmetic differences.

Possible cells:

- low subscription / high setup;
- high monthly with human-in-loop;
- fixed 90-day engagement;
- software-only lower tier once intelligence can be automated.

Do not infer willingness to pay from survey ratings alone.

# 17. Stop Rule result

## O recalled

Authority / market-position outcome remains the current end-to-end product telos.

## Material replan

The system belongs architecturally to Personal Decision Intelligence, but broadening the commercial product to generic personal decisions would reduce price power, domain specificity and natural retention.

## Current category hierarchy

```text
ARCHITECTURAL CATEGORY
Personal Decision Intelligence
        ↓
FIRST VERTICAL
Professional Authority / Market Position
        ↓
INITIAL ICP
Independent experts / consultants / fractional leaders in a professional transition
        ↓
PAID JOB
Improve representation + resource allocation across a continuing authority journey
        ↓
RETENTION
New decision / evidence / outcome / dependency triggers + compounding decision lineage
```

## Current outcome

`REPLAN → FIELD`

No broader product build is justified.

The next highest-value evidence is real paid Wizard-of-Oz use across multiple decision cycles, not another generic decision framework or broader feature set.
