# Research Grounding v3

## Purpose

This note records external evidence that informs, but does not govern, the current product architecture.

The purpose is not to turn research into product dogma. It is to distinguish:

- externally supported mechanisms;
- product hypotheses;
- claims that still require field validation with the actual ICP.

Telos Governance remains the highest authority.

---

# 1. Personal branding is strategic, targeted and programmatic

Gorbatov, Khapova and Lysova (2018), *Personal Branding: Interdisciplinary Systematic Review and Research Agenda*, synthesized 100 academic publications.

Relevant findings for this product:

- personal branding is treated as a strategic activity directed at a defined audience;
- effective positioning involves both fit with field expectations and meaningful differentiation;
- common process elements include self-awareness, needs analysis and positioning, constructing brand architecture, feedback-seeking and sensemaking;
- the literature distinguishes desired identity from perceived identity;
- the audience's actual perception is especially important because it shapes action toward the person.

### Product implication

An authority-building product should not stop at self-description or content creation.

It needs:

- explicit target audience;
- current-state understanding;
- desired perceived identity;
- field/competitor context;
- positioning and differentiation;
- feedback from reality;
- adaptation over time.

### Limitation

The personal-branding literature is heterogeneous and the review itself notes fragmented theory. These findings justify mechanisms to test, not a universal authority formula.

# 2. Reputation is audience-relative and "known for something" matters

Kamp and Graf-Vlachy (2024/2025), *Strategic leader reputation: a review and research agenda*, synthesize research on strategic leader reputation.

Relevant findings:

- the "being known for something" dimension appears as a central dimension in individual reputation definitions;
- reputation assessments differ by audience;
- visible actions, business practices, external recognition, media/intermediaries and context can affect reputation;
- reputation effects are not always immediate or direct;
- reputation can influence trust and downstream judgments.

### Product implication

"Authority" should not be represented as one universal scalar.

The system should ask:

- known by whom?
- known for what?
- based on which signals?
- through which intermediaries?
- with what observed downstream behavior?

### Limitation

Strategic-leader research often studies CEOs/directors and may not transfer directly to independent experts. The product must test transferability.

# 3. Thought leadership can affect B2B vetting and receptivity

LinkedIn and Edelman's 2024 B2B Thought Leadership research reports that decision-makers use thought leadership in assessing providers and that high-quality thought leadership can increase research, trust/receptivity and consideration.

The report emphasizes characteristics such as:

- grounding in credible research/data;
- helping buyers understand important business challenges/opportunities;
- concrete guidance and cases;
- capturing attention early.

### Product implication

Content is a legitimate authority-building mechanism, especially for B2B experts, but its value is not "posting" itself.

The strategic system should connect content to:

- audience problem;
- useful insight / methodology;
- credible support;
- actual downstream response.

### Limitation

This is industry-sponsored survey research and describes thought leadership at organization/company level as well as individuals. Treat magnitude estimates as market evidence rather than scientific causal estimates for this product.

# 4. Brand building is a system, not a publication event

Fluhrer and Brahm (2025/2026), *How small businesses build their brands in a digital world: a systematic review*, synthesize empirical SME-branding research.

Their framework groups research into:

- brand orientation;
- brand identity;
- brand marketing;
- brand performance.

The review also notes the value of market orientation and competitor benchmarking and reports that effects often operate indirectly through identity, resources and communication.

### Product implication

The product should not collapse strategy into content production.

A plausible authority system needs to connect:

- strategic orientation;
- identity / positioning;
- assets and resources;
- market-facing activity;
- performance / feedback.

### Limitation

SME brand research concerns firms as well as founder-linked brands. It supports system structure more strongly than exact individual-level mechanisms.

# 5. Current integrated mechanism hypothesis

The combined evidence supports the following working architecture:

```text
Desired authority position
→ target audience / field
→ self + existing capital/assets
→ positioning / differentiation
→ authority assets and public signals
→ distribution / intermediaries / repeated exposure
→ audience perception
→ market / business behavior
→ feedback and sensemaking
→ revised strategy
```

The product adds a strategic orchestration layer:

```text
current state + field state
→ adaptive diagnosis
→ dependency-aware map
→ highest-leverage next action
→ execution
→ observed signal
→ learning
```

This orchestration layer is a product hypothesis. The literature does not establish that an AI system can reliably generate the optimal authority route.

That claim must be field-tested.

---

# 6. What remains explicitly unproven

Do not treat the following as established facts:

- that one universal set of authority dimensions exists;
- that Natural / Commercial / Fast is the optimal audience-choice framing;
- that the system can infer causal authority-building mechanisms from public expert histories;
- that more content increases authority;
- that a dependency graph of authority actions can be reliably generated without human correction;
- that the same authority-building route works across industries;
- that users will trust automatic public-footprint analysis;
- that users will continue supplying data because strategic-resolution gamification is motivating;
- that observed inbound or deals can be attributed to a specific authority action;
- that users will pay for the ongoing strategic map rather than only for immediate output generation.

These are FIELD questions.

---

# 7. Research-use rule

External research should be fetched when it can change a product decision.

Do not add frameworks or dimensions merely because literature contains them.

For every research-derived addition ask:

> Which user action, system action, dependency, evidence requirement or field experiment changes because of this finding?

If the answer is "none", the finding does not enter product doctrine.
